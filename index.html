About

Hi there, I am Shiye Du (杜是也), an undergraduate student majoring in Chemistry at Westlake University. My academic training spans chemistry, nanoscience, and machine learning, with a current focus on building physically interpretable design principles for plasmonic nanostructures.

Since January 2025, I have been working with Prof. Hongyu Chen in the School of Science at Westlake University, with my research co-supervised by Prof. Mouhong Lin at the College of Optical Science and Engineering, Zhejiang University. My current research centers on three directions: universal nanoparticle descriptors for physics-aware inverse design, optical force and torque enhancement in coupled plasmonic systems, and thermally switchable multishell plasmonic nanomachines.

Before joining Prof. Chen's lab, I conducted undergraduate research with Prof. Jianjun Cheng, studying rapid NCA ring-opening polymerization methods through monomer synthesis, polypeptide synthesis, and FTIR-based kinetic characterization.

I also completed one semester of academic study as a visiting student at Duke University in Fall 2025. I am broadly interested in combining simulation, descriptor design, and deep learning to make nanoscale materials and machines more interpretable, generalizable, and experimentally actionable.
